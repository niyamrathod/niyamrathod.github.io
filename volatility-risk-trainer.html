Project 01

Volatility, Sizing
& Risk Trainer

Reads an asset's volatility, asks you to size and position a trade, then stress-tests it through a real crisis and grades your risk process, not whether you called the market. Runs entirely in the browser.

What this taught me about markets

The biggest thing was realising that risk is something you can actually control, but direction isn't. I can size a trade to a set amount of risk and check whether it survives a crash, but I can't know which way the market will go. Giving marks for a lucky call would just be hindsight. It also made the link between volatility and sizing concrete: how much a thing moves is what should decide how big you trade it, not a number you pick yourself.

Why I built this

I had two separate tools. An options and volatility one, and a position-sizing one that ran on coin flips. Neither felt like how a real decision actually gets made. So I merged them into one loop: you read an asset's volatility, decide how big to go and which way, then a real crisis hits and it tells you what you did well and what you got wrong.

What I'd still improve

Right now it only handles one asset at a time. I'd add a second next: in a calm market holding two things looks like it spreads your risk, but in a crisis they tend to fall together, so the real risk is bigger than it looks. The crises here are also based on rough historical moves rather than live data.

Step 1: Volatility
Realized vs implied vol. Realized (RV) is how much the asset has actually been moving; implied (IV) is what the options market is pricing for the future. When IV sits well above RV, the market is paying up for protection. Treat that as a signal to size down. The 1-day 95% VaR shown is per unit of position: roughly daily-vol × 1.645, the loss you'd exceed only 1 day in 20.
Step 2: Your decision
Positioning
Sizing off volatility, not gut. Your position's 1-day 95% VaR is notional × daily-vol × 1.645. The disciplined size is the one that makes that VaR equal to your risk budget. Bigger vol means a smaller position for the same risk. That is the whole idea the coin-flip version missed: the stake isn't a free dial, it's set by how much the thing moves.
Step 3: Stress test & correction
Simulated P&L paths for your position over the ~30-day event at this regime's volatility. Shaded = middle 50% / 90% of outcomes; dark line = median; red dashed = your stop; the dot marks what this specific historical crisis actually did to your position.

Scorecard: risk process
Why process, not P&L. In a scripted crisis the outcome is known, so it would be easy to reward "you should have been short." That just trains hindsight. What actually repeats is discipline: sizing to a budget, cutting into rising vol, keeping a single loss survivable, and knowing a stop can gap. Those are the things scored above.
