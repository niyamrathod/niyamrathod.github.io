Project 01

Volatility &
Derivatives Toolkit

I built this to understand how options are actually priced and where implied vol comes from. Pick a stock below and you can see how the surface forms across strikes and expiries, back out the IV from any price, and run a rough straddle scenario. Still learning the finer details of how the skew behaves around events.

  • Python
  • Black-Scholes
  • Options Pricing
  • Implied Volatility
  • Gradio
  • Quantitative Finance
What this taught me about markets

Implied volatility is the market's consensus forecast of future moves, but building the surface showed me where that consensus breaks down. The skew shifts a lot more around macro events than I expected — I didn't fully appreciate how much IV reprices around FOMC or CPI before I could see it moving in the data.

Why I built this

I kept reading about implied vol without really understanding how a single number gets extracted from a market price. Building the Brent's method inversion made it concrete — you're just solving Black-Scholes backwards. The 3D surface came later, once I wanted to see how IV varies across strikes and tenors rather than just at one point.

What I'd still improve

The model assumes constant vol (Black-Scholes), so the surface is really just plotting market-implied deviations from that assumption rather than a true stochastic vol model. I'd want to understand SABR or Heston before treating any of these numbers as production-grade.

Best experienced on desktop or laptop.
