Project 04

Position Sizing
& Risk Lab

Every game here has a guaranteed edge. The only variable is how much you stake. Change any input and every chart updates. Built to learn sizing, drawdown and ruin by watching them move.

  • Risk Management
  • Position Sizing
  • Kelly Criterion
  • Monte Carlo

Why I built this

At Deutsche Bank I saw VaR and stress testing used in practice without really understanding what was inside the numbers. What stuck with me afterwards was reading that traders fail more often from sizing badly than from being wrong about direction, which I didn't really believe. This was the simplest experiment I could design to test it: hold the edge constant, change only the stake, and watch what happens across thousands of simulated lifetimes. It runs entirely in the browser because the idea doesn't need any data.

What this taught me about markets

The mean is a liar: a few lucky paths drag the average up while the typical outcome is far worse, so positive expected value alone tells you almost nothing about what happens to you. Over-betting turns a winning game into a losing one. Same edge, same coin. Past a certain stake the median lifetime ends in ruin. And the punishment for sitting above the Kelly stake is far worse than the cost of sitting below it, which is why desks risk around 1% per trade when the maths might "allow" ten times that.

What I'd still improve

Real returns aren't independent coin flips: fat tails and clustered losing streaks make real over-betting more dangerous than this shows. Kelly also assumes you know your edge, which in markets you never do. Modelling that estimation error is what I'd want to add next. It's a teaching simulator, not a backtest, and shouldn't be read as one.

Loss tail of ending capital, with VaR and Expected Shortfall marked.
VaR answers a simple question: how bad is a bad case? The 95% VaR is the loss you only exceed 5% of the time. Expected Shortfall (CVaR) asks the better one: when it IS that bad, how bad on average? Desks use both because VaR says nothing about what lives beyond it.
Every rule below trades the identical sequence of coin flips. Only the sizing differs. That is the whole experiment: same edge, same luck, different survival.
Fixed fraction = same % of current bankroll (shrinks after losses, which is why it survives). Fixed stake = same £ amount every bet. Kelly / half-Kelly = growth-optimal and the practitioner's discount. Vol-target = smaller stake when recent results are choppy.
Losing streaks are a mathematical certainty, not bad luck. Sizing decides whether the inevitable streak is a bruise or a funeral.
