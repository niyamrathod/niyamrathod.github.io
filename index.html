What I'm Watching

A few markets I've been following and trying to form a view on. Not formal trade calls, just things I'm thinking about. Illustrative only. Not investment advice.

Market coverage: Reuters Markets  ·  FT Markets  ·  Bloomberg

Rates Watching

US Rates

The curve un-inverted in late 2025 when the Fed cut three times and the short end fell faster. What I'm watching now is the opposite risk: whether this Fed ends up hiking again.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Aug 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% in July, but three policymakers dissented and Warsh gave almost no forward guidance
  • Around 65% odds priced for a September hike
  • 2-year at 4.21%, 10-year at 4.63%. 2s10s is around +42bp

What I think

  • The 2-year sitting above the Fed's own rate means hikes are already priced. So the question isn't whether they hike, but whether they hike more than expected
  • I lean towards more: inflation is still above target and this Fed has put price stability ahead of employment
  • What would change my mind: core inflation cooling towards 2%, or hiring falling away sharply. One removes the reason to hike, the other the willingness
FX Watching

USD/JPY

The BoJ has been hiking while the Fed stopped cutting, and I expected that narrowing gap to bring the yen back. It didn't. Then last week it moved for a completely different reason.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Aug 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • The dollar was above 163 yen late last week, near 40-year highs
  • Japan and the US then launched a rare joint yen-buying intervention on 3 August. The dollar fell about 1% to 156.34 and sits near 157 today
  • Japan had already spent a record ¥11.7 trillion (~$73bn) intervening in April and May. The pair was back above that level within six weeks

What I think

  • Right on direction, wrong on mechanism. The yen strengthened because two governments bought it, not because the gap closed
  • Intervention moves the price, not the cause. Japan's finance ministry can't change interest rates, so unless the gap narrows the same pressure rebuilds. April and May proved that
  • What's new is that this one is joint with the US, and coordinated intervention has historically lasted longer than one country acting alone. That's what I'm watching, not the level
  • What would tell me the pressure isn't rebuilding: the BoJ actually delivering more hikes while the Fed holds. Then the yen wouldn't need defending
Commodities Watching

Oil

My original thesis broke when OPEC+ started adding supply. What's interesting now is that July tested my own lesson that war premiums fade fast, because this time actual barrels stopped moving.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Aug 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • Brent hit $120 in April on Iran tensions, then round-tripped completely
  • July was different: US strikes resumed, the Strait of Hormuz was largely closed (~20% of seaborne oil), and Brent rose over 20% into the high $80s
  • Brent is around $81 now, after dropping 5% on 3 August when Trump said talks would resume and predicted the strait reopening. Iran denies direct talks

What I think

  • April faded because barrels kept flowing. That was a fear premium. July was a physical supply loss, and it held far longer
  • So I'd stop treating every geopolitical spike the same way. Headlines fade; missing barrels don't
  • What I watch instead of the news: whether the curve stays in backwardation (buyers paying more for oil now than later, which only happens in a real shortage) and whether tankers are moving through the strait. If both normalise while headlines stay loud, the premium is going
Digital Assets Watching

Bitcoin

Bitcoin halved in 2024 and the US spot ETFs opened it to a much bigger pool of buyers. I followed it expecting the halving to matter, and learned that flows took over from it.

I follow this one out of interest rather than conviction. I don't have a view I'd defend the way I would the others.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Aug 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • Around $64k, roughly half its late-2025 peak
  • June was the worst month on record for the ETFs at $4.51bn of outflows. July was mixed; early August has seen about $132m of inflows across four sessions
  • Research suggests ETF flows explain roughly 45% of weekly price moves

What I think

  • The flows idea is now mainstream, which is exactly why I wouldn't call it an edge
  • Recent bounces came from macro (softer inflation, weaker dollar), not anything crypto-specific
  • I'm not going to pretend to a strong view here. It's the market I follow most casually and I'd rather say so
