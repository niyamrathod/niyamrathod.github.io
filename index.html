What I'm Watching

A few markets I've been following and trying to form a view on. Not formal trade calls, just things I'm thinking about. Illustrative only. Not investment advice.

Market coverage: Reuters Markets  ·  FT Markets  ·  Bloomberg

Rates Watching

US Rates

For most of the time I've followed this, short-term US rates were above long-term ones — an inverted curve. That finally flipped back in late 2025 when the Fed cut three times and the short end fell faster, which is roughly the mechanism I expected. Now I'm trying to work out what happens next.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Jul 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • The Fed kept rates at 3.50–3.75% in June, but their own forecasts now show fewer cuts coming — and a few members even talked about raising rates again
  • The gap between short and long-term rates keeps widening: 2s10s is around +35–40bp, and the 10-year is near 4.6%

What I think

  • My steepening idea has mostly worked so far — long-term yields went up as people stopped expecting cuts, which was the exact thing I was betting on
  • The risk I wrote down (the Fed hiking again) is actually being talked about now, so I'm less confident than I was. Watching this one closely
FX Watching

USD/JPY

Japan's central bank has been raising rates for the first time in decades while the Fed is moving the other way, and I think that shift in the rate gap between the two countries should bring the yen back up over time. The yen has been weak for years partly because Japanese rates were near zero, so this feels like a real structural change, though I'd want to see the BoJ actually follow through consistently before being too confident.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Jul 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • The BoJ raised rates again to 1% — the highest since 1995 — and their governor is hinting at more hikes
  • Even so, USD/JPY is still stuck around 162, near 40-year highs

What I think

  • This one confuses me in a really interesting way. The rate gap is closing like I expected, but the yen still won't move — I think it means the market just doesn't believe the BoJ yet
  • I still think I'm right about the direction, but I've given up guessing the timing. Big lesson: being right eventually isn't the same as being right on time
Commodities Watching

Oil

I've been watching oil grind higher. OPEC is still holding back supply and demand from Asia keeps coming in stronger than expected, so I think there's more room to run. Though I'd want to see how long OPEC actually holds discipline before being too convicted, and a sharper slowdown in China would probably change the picture quickly.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Jul 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • Brent shot up to $120 in April when tensions with Iran blocked shipping, then fell all the way back down once things calmed
  • OPEC+ just agreed to add more supply again — another +188k b/d from August, the fifth month in a row

What I think

  • Really glad I had my exit rule written down — OPEC keeps adding barrels, and even a real supply shock couldn't keep the price up
  • The April spike taught me something: war premiums in oil fade way faster than I thought. Watching from the sidelines for now
Digital Assets Watching

Bitcoin

Bitcoin's supply got cut in half back in 2024. Every four years or so the amount of new coins minted drops automatically, and historically that has been a meaningful driver of the price in the months after. The new US spot ETFs have also opened it up to a much bigger pool of buyers. I'm aware it tends to sell off with equities when markets get nervous, so timing feels important.

Read FT  Reuters  Bloomberg 
Updated Jul 2026Full pitch →

What's happening

  • Price is in the mid-$60ks, about half of its late-2025 peak
  • June was the worst month ever for the ETFs — $4.5bn pulled out. Flows turned positive for a bit in early July, but the year is still ~$5.4bn negative overall

What I think

  • The flows idea I had is now basically mainstream — some research says ETF flows explain around 45% of weekly price moves, which I find fascinating
  • The recent bounces came from macro stuff (soft inflation data, weak dollar), not anything crypto-specific. The halving story feels like old news now
