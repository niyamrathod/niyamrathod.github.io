Project 05

Trade Pitch
Builder

I built this to force myself to structure a proper view — thesis, instrument, levels, and where I'd be wrong — before treating an opinion as a trade idea. Three pitches I've worked through properly are featured below, plus a few earlier practice attempts across other asset classes. Still very much learning how to do this well.

What this taught me about markets

I thought the hard part would be forming the view — it turned out to be the opposite. Getting specific about what would actually make me change my mind was much harder than I expected. I kept writing exit conditions that were too vague to be useful. Working through pitches across different asset classes also made clear how differently each one works and how much I still don't know about most of them.

Method

How to Structure a Pitch

The way I try to structure a pitch has six parts. If I skip one, the idea tends to stay too vague to be useful — no entry, no sense of what's at risk, nothing I can be concretely wrong about. I use this as a checklist to pressure-test ideas before I get too attached to them.

01

View

The thesis

Try to state what is mispriced, the mechanism that closes the gap, and why now — not just a direction. What I've found useful is making the view falsifiable: "the front end reprices cuts faster than the back end once hiking stops" works as a view; "rates go down" doesn't.

02

Instrument

How you express it

The same view can be right and the trade still lose money if the instrument is wrong. I try to specify exactly: the security, tenor, strike or expiry, and why this structure captures the move better than the obvious alternatives.

03

Sizing & Levels

Entry, target, stop

Without precise levels, a pitch tends to stay too vague to act on. I try to set entry, target, and stop before the trade goes on — not after it moves against me. Size should reflect conviction and risk tolerance, not gut feel.

04

Risk / Reward

The math

Reward divided by risk, in the same units, before transaction costs. I also try to state the breakeven explicitly — what move does the market need to make for the trade to pay off? The rule of thumb I've landed on is looking for at least 1.5:1 before costs.

05

Catalyst

What moves it

I try to distinguish between rationale (why I think I'm right) and catalyst (what actually makes the market move). A catalyst is specific and time-bound — a data print, a central bank decision, an earnings call. Without one, the idea can be correct for years and still not pay off.

06

What Kills It

The risk that ends the trade

I've found that if I can't articulate a clear bear case, the trade probably isn't ready. Knowing what kills it is what lets me set a stop that makes sense rather than one I picked arbitrarily — and makes it easier to exit when the thesis actually breaks.

Worked Examples

Three Pitches

The three pitches I've worked through most carefully. Each one uses the six-field structure from above — these are the cases where I feel I can at least partially defend every field.

01

2s10s DV01-Neutral Steepener

01 View
The 2s10s spread is inverted at around −28 bps, well below its post-crisis average of around +100 bps. If the Fed begins cutting rates, short-end yields should fall faster than the long end — pushing the curve back toward a more normal shape.
02 Instrument
DV01-neutral: long 2yr UST futures / short 10yr UST futures in a ratio that equalises the dollar sensitivity of each leg. This isolates the curve shape from overall rate direction — the position is right or wrong on the curve shape, not on the level of rates.
05 Catalyst
First Fed rate cut, or a payrolls or CPI print that brings the cutting cycle materially forward.
06 What Kills It
Fed holds higher for longer; or term premium in the long end rises on fiscal / supply concerns, steepening from the wrong side.

01 View
Tech has outperformed consumer discretionary by a wide margin and I think the underlying drivers still hold. AI capex spend continues to benefit XLK; XLY faces softer consumers as excess savings have largely run out and credit card delinquencies have been creeping higher.
02 Instrument
Long XLK (Technology SPDR ETF) / Short XLY (Consumer Discretionary SPDR ETF), dollar-neutral at roughly 1.42:1. The pair removes broad equity beta so the position is specifically a view on the spread between the two sectors, not a directional equity bet.
05 Catalyst
Weak consumer confidence, rising delinquency data, or a soft payrolls print that hits discretionary names harder than tech.
06 What Kills It
AI capex pullback or a major tech earnings miss; or a strong consumer data surprise that lifts XLY more than XLK.

01 View
OPEC+ has been maintaining supply discipline and Asian demand — particularly from China — has held up better than pessimists expected. At around $77/bbl Brent seems to already price in a lot of the downside. The curve is in backwardation, which means there is a positive roll yield while holding a long position.
02 Instrument
Long ICE Brent futures (BRN front month) or USO ETF. I'd lean toward the futures contract where backwardation means positive roll yield each month while holding — something an ETF does not fully pass through.
05 Catalyst
OPEC+ compliance data confirming cuts hold; stronger China demand data; a geopolitical supply disruption (windfall, not part of the thesis).
06 What Kills It
OPEC+ fractures and members overproduce; China demand disappoints; US production rises faster than expected.
Earlier Attempts

More Practice Examples

Rougher or less developed pitches across a few more asset classes. Useful for seeing how the structure applies differently in each market, but I'd put less weight on the specific levels here.

Volatility

Short SPY Straddle

Practice pitch on selling implied vol when the SPY implied/realised gap looked wide. The main learning was how much the P&L depends on being right about vol timing, not just direction.

FX

G10 Carry Basket

Long AUD/JPY and NZD/JPY carry basket. The carry logic made sense, but thinking through where the stop goes and how carry unwinds can happen fast and together was harder than I expected.

Inflation / TIPS

Long 5yr Breakeven

Buying inflation expectations via TIPS vs nominal Treasuries. Getting comfortable with how breakevens move differently from outright rates was the main learning here.

Digital Assets

Long BTC/USD

Halving-supply thesis with ETF flow tailwind. The hardest field to fill in was "what kills it" — BTC tends to sell off with equities in risk-off episodes, which made me think harder about how to frame the stop around correlation risk.
