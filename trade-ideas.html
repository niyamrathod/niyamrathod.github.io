Method

How to Structure a Pitch

Every trade pitch has six components. Miss one and the trade becomes an opinion — it has no entry, no defined risk, and no clear thesis to break. The structure below applies across asset classes: rates, FX, equities, or derivatives.

01

View

The thesis

State what is mispriced, the mechanism that closes the gap, and why now — not just a direction. A good view is falsifiable: "the front end reprices faster than the back end once hiking stops" is a view. "Rates go down" is not.

02

Instrument

How you express it

The same view can be right and the trade still lose money if the instrument is wrong. Specify exactly the security, tenor, strike or expiry, and why this structure captures the view better than the alternatives.

03

Sizing & Levels

Entry, target, stop

A pitch without precise levels is an opinion. Set your entry, target, and stop before the trade goes on — not after it moves against you. Size should reflect conviction and risk budget, not instinct.

04

Risk / Reward

The math

Reward divided by risk, in the same units, before transaction costs. State the breakeven: what move does the market need to make for the trade to break even? A good trade has R/R above 1.5:1.

05

Catalyst

What moves it

Distinguish between rationale (why you are right) and catalyst (what makes the market move). A catalyst is specific and time-bound — a data print, a central bank decision, an earnings call. Without one, the trade can be right for years before it pays.

06

What Kills It

The risk that ends the trade

If you cannot articulate a clear bear case, you do not understand the trade. Knowing what kills it lets you set your stop logically, exit quickly when the thesis breaks, and avoid holding a position past the point where it makes sense.

Example Pitches

01

2s10s DV01-Neutral Steepener

Rates & Yield Curve tool Open

View

The 2s10s spread is inverted at −28bps, inside its 20-year average of +100bps. The Fed has paused its hiking cycle; historically, steepeners outperform in the 6–12 months following the final hike as the front end reprices cuts ahead of the long end re-anchoring to nominal growth. The 5yr5yr inflation forward at 2.4% suggests the term premium has room to rebuild. The Rates & Yield Curve tool shows the curve is flatter than at any point since 2006 on a DV01-adjusted basis.

Instrument

DV01-neutral: long $10m notional 2yr UST (DV01 $186/bp) against short $3.4m 10yr UST (DV01 $541/bp). Size ratio 2.9:1 long-to-short, keeping net DV01 below $50/bp. Expressed in cash Treasuries; CME Treasury futures available for capital efficiency.

Sizing & Levels

Entry: −28bps (2yr 4.82%, 10yr 4.54%)
Target: −8bps (+20bp gain)
Stop: −42bps (−14bp loss)
DV01/bp: ~$50
P&L at target: +$1,000
P&L at stop: −$700

Risk / Reward & Breakevens

Reward: +20bp × $50/bp = +$1,000
Risk: −14bp × $50/bp = −$700
R/R: 1.4 : 1
Daily carry cost: ~−$6 (short duration vs funding)
Carry-adjusted breakeven: ~12bp widening over 30 days

Catalyst

FOMC minutes signalling rate cuts in H1 2026; below-consensus NFP (<150k) forcing a pull-forward in cut pricing; or a Treasury supply announcement that steepens from the long end.

What Kills It

A second inflation wave forces the Fed to resume hiking, keeping the front end elevated and re-inverting the curve. A global growth shock triggers a bull-flattening safe-haven bid into long-end Treasuries. Foreign central bank demand (BoJ normalisation) disproportionately compresses the 10yr yield, flattening from the back end.

02

Short SPY 490 Dec Straddle

Volatility & Derivatives Toolkit Target Hit

View

SPY 30-day implied vol at 21.4% is 1.5× the trailing 30-day realised vol of 14.2% — an IV/RV spread of 7.2 points, at the 74th percentile over 5 years. The IV surface from the Volatility & Derivatives Toolkit shows the front-month skew is elevated, pricing excessive near-term tail risk. Post-FOMC meetings in non-crisis years show a median IV crush of 3–4 points within 5 sessions. Selling premium here captures the vol risk premium and benefits from accelerating theta decay.

Instrument

Sell SPY 490 Dec straddle (28 DTE):
Sell 1× 490 call at $9.20
Sell 1× 490 put at $9.05
Net premium received: $18.25/share ($1,825/contract)
Initial delta: −0.02 (approximately neutral)
Vega: −$42 per vol point

Sizing & Levels

Strike: $490 (ATM)
Expiry: 19 Dec 2025 (28 DTE)
Contracts: 5
Total premium received: $9,125
Position size: ~4% of book
Manage at 50% profit ($4,562) or 200% loss ($18,250)

Risk / Reward & Breakevens

Upper breakeven: $508.25
Lower breakeven: $471.75
Max gain: $9,125 (SPY at exactly $490 at expiry)
Theta: +$162/day
IV crush to take 50% profit: 21% → ~16%
R/R at 50%/200% manage points: 1 : 2 (offset by positive theta)

Catalyst

FOMC meeting passes without surprise; implied vol reverts toward the trailing 30-day realised of 14%; equity market consolidates within 3% of $490 for 2+ weeks, maximising theta decay.

What Kills It

SPY moves beyond either breakeven (more than 3.7% in either direction). A macro shock — an unexpected inflation print, a geopolitical event — keeps IV elevated or pushes it higher, making the short vega position lose money even without a directional move. Deep in-the-money assignment risk near expiry if one leg becomes ITM and the other expires worthless.

03

G10 FX Carry Basket

FX Carry & CIP tool Stopped

View

G10 rate differentials are at multi-decade wides: USD/JPY at 5.3%, AUD/JPY at 5.0%, NZD/JPY at 4.7%. VIX has held below 16 for 8 consecutive weeks, keeping FX realised vol suppressed. The FX Carry & CIP tool backtest over 3 years returns 11.2% p.a. at 8.6% vol (Sharpe: 1.30) in comparable volatility regimes. CIP deviation on AUD/USD 1-month forward: +14bps, providing an additional funding edge on top of the carry.

Instrument

Long: AUD/USD (0.6520), NZD/USD (0.5980), USD/CAD (1.3560 — long CAD)
Short: EUR/USD (1.0840), USD/CHF (0.8930 — short CHF), USD/JPY (149.50 — short JPY)
Equal-weighted at $50k per leg via 1-month FX forwards to lock carry income.

Sizing & Levels

Notional per leg: $50,000
Total gross: $300,000 ($150k long, $150k short)
Stop: −$4,500 (−3% on gross, ~0.9% per leg)
Take profit: +$8,000 (+5.3% on gross, ~1× annualised carry)
Review at 1-month forward expiry

Risk / Reward & Breakevens

Annualised carry income: 4.9% ($14,700/yr on gross)
Daily carry accrual: ~$40
Expected basket vol: 8.3% p.a.
Carry-to-vol ratio: 0.59×
Carry covers a 1.4% adverse basket move before losses

Catalyst

US ISM Manufacturing holds above 50; Fed maintains pause language; JPY unwind fears recede after BoJ reaffirms gradual normalisation path; no EM shock that spills into G10 via risk-off correlation.

What Kills It

A sudden risk-off event forces simultaneous JPY and CHF short squeezes, taking the two largest short positions against us at the same time. A BoJ surprise rate hike mechanically unwinds USD/JPY carry across the market. An equity drawdown above 8% triggers a global risk-off unwind, compressing all carry positions regardless of fundamentals — this is precisely the scenario the trade stopped on in September 2025.
