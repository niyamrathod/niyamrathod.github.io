A physical trader's screen built from my BP crude & gas fundamentals work: a zoomable world map of tanker and LNG traffic through the key chokepoints, the forward curve (contango vs backwardation) with grade and regional spreads, and storage-vs-5-year-range fundamentals. What this taught me about markets

Building this made the physical side click: a commodity's price is really about whether the actual barrel or cargo can get to where it's needed. Storage tells you if the market is tight or drowning, chokepoints decide whether supply can even move, and the shape of the forward curve is the market saying which of those it believes. A full tank or a blocked strait moves things more than a scary headline that fades.

Why I built this

After my BP internship modelling crude and gas supply-demand, I wanted one screen that put the whole physical picture in one place, the ships, the storage, and the curve, instead of three tabs that never talked to each other. The map is the way in; the fundamentals and the curve are the substance behind it.

What I'd still improve

The vessels and prices here are illustrative so the page runs offline. The live version plugs into AISStream for real ship positions and the EIA and European gas-storage APIs for the fundamentals, refreshed on the same GitHub Action that runs my morning brief. Cargo contents and freight rates are limited on free data, so the map speaks in vessel types, not what's inside the hull.

Illustrative positions; the live build streams real AIS from aisstream.io filtered to these regions.
Hover or click a point on the curve for month detail.
Forward curve, front month to a year out. Backwardation (front above back) says the market is short right now and paying up for prompt barrels; contango (back above front) says it's oversupplied and worth storing. The front–back spread is the number a physical trader watches.
Signals (illustrative price history)
Trend and momentum are price-based; the mean-reversion z-score asks how stretched the front is versus its own range. Read it next to the fundamentals tab, because "rich" during a real shortage is not the same as an overshoot.
Grade & regional spreads
Brent vs WTI is quality and location; Brent vs Dubai is Atlantic vs Gulf crude; the gas spreads (Henry Hub vs TTF vs JKM) are what decide whether an LNG cargo sails to Europe or Asia.
How the market reacted to past shocks
Click an event on the timeline.
Inventories against their 5-year range. Below the average band and falling means tight; up against the top means oversupplied.
Storage economics in one view: where stocks sit versus history, how many days of demand they cover, and net trade as a stand-in for deliveries.
The through-line. Fundamentals and storage set how tight the market is, that tightness shows up as the shape of the forward curve, and the map is where you see whether the physical barrels can actually move to relieve it. Same chain a desk reasons along, in one screen.
