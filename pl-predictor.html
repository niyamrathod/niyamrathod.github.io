Project 06

PL 2026/27
Predictor

A Dixon-Coles model I fitted to four seasons of Premier League results to predict match outcomes. I built it partly because football data is clean and easy to work with, and partly because it forced me to actually implement a probability model from scratch. Five tools sit on top: a Transfer Machine, a Smart Signing optimiser, a Model vs Market edge board comparing my probabilities to bookmaker odds, a Track Record scoring tab, and 2026/27 season tracking that scores predictions as results come in.

  • Monte Carlo
  • Dixon-Coles
  • Transfer Machine
  • Smart Signing
  • Model vs Market
  • Python
What this taught me about markets

The Model vs Market tab was the most interesting part. Comparing my model probabilities to bookmaker odds made me realise how much information is already priced in. The model disagrees with the market sometimes, but not consistently enough to feel like a real edge. I think that's actually a useful thing to learn about what it takes to beat a well-functioning market.

Why I built this

I wanted to build something that involved real probability and statistics rather than just reading about it. Football felt like a good test case: the outcomes are messy and hard to predict, there's four seasons of data to fit on, and I could actually check whether the model worked after the fact. It also just seemed like a more interesting project than a standard stock-price predictor.

What I'd still improve

The model doesn't account for injuries, new signings, or form streaks. It treats each team's attack and defence as fixed for the season, which I know isn't right. The ratings also get stale as the season progresses and I update them manually. I'd want a longer out-of-sample period before reading too much into the Track Record numbers.

Best experienced on desktop or laptop.
